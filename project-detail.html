Spirit Bound

Spirit Bound is a 5-level pixel-art adventure platformer developed as a five-person university project, following Boo, a playful ghost on a quest to regain his humanity. As Lead Animator and designer for the climactic final stage, I designed and implemented the Level 5 boss fight against Mia, a spell-casting cat. My contributions covered the complete feature pipeline, including character sprites and animations created in Aseprite, custom combat interactions, and the boss defeat sequence.

Problem

I wanted to strengthen my 3D modeling skills by creating a complete themed environment rather than focusing on a single object.

The challenge was to model a variety of characters and props with distinct shapes, materials, and visual details while maintaining a consistent art direction across the entire scene.

I also wanted to explore how lighting, composition, and simple animations could add personality and atmosphere to the final environment.

Solution

I developed a stylized circus environment featuring multiple original 3D assets, including a circus tent, clown, lion, elephant, rabbit in a magician's hat, cannon, decorative props, and environmental elements.

Using Blender, I modeled each asset, applied materials and textures, adjusted lighting, and arranged the elements into a cohesive scene.

I also experimented with keyframe animation, adding movement to selected objects to bring additional energy and personality to the environment.

Visual Direction

The project follows a playful, cartoon-inspired visual style, combining bright colors and pastel tones to create a cheerful, welcoming atmosphere.

I used rounded geometric forms, exaggerated proportions, and simplified character features to establish a consistent visual identity across the different models.

Warm lighting, soft shadows, and contrasting materials helped emphasize the shapes and details of each asset, while the final composition brought the individual elements together into a festive circus setting.

The Cast

02 — Character & Prop Development

I created several distinct characters and objects, including a clown, lion, elephant, rabbit, and cat, alongside circus props such as a tent, cannon, and decorative structures.

Each asset presented different modeling challenges, from defining character proportions and facial features to creating more intricate geometry and decorative details.

This stage allowed me to practice building recognizable, expressive forms while maintaining a consistent art style.

From Primitives to Final Model

01 — 3D Modeling

I modeled the characters, structures, and environmental props in Blender using primitive meshes such as spheres, cylinders, cones, and cubes.

Through mesh editing, scaling, extrusion, and geometry modifiers, I transformed these basic shapes into stylized characters and objects.

I explored different modeling techniques, including Subdivision Surface for smoother forms, Mirror for symmetrical geometry, and mesh-editing tools for more detailed structures.

Materials, Lighting and Scene

03 — Materials & Lighting

I applied materials and textures to establish visual differences between the models, experimenting with color, surface finishes, and reflectivity.

I used contrasting colors, metallic finishes, and textured surfaces to give the assets distinct appearances.

I then adjusted the scene lighting to emphasize forms, create soft shadows, and establish the atmosphere of the final environment.

04 — Scene Composition & Rendering

Once the individual assets were completed, I arranged them into a unified circus environment, combining characters, props, lighting, and decorative elements.

I focused on the placement of objects, visual balance, and the relationship between foreground and background elements to create a cohesive composition.

I rendered both individual assets and the assembled scene to showcase the models, materials, and overall visual direction.

Problem

Caring for a pet means keeping track of many small things: vet visits, vaccines, weight, grooming, symptoms, and care advice that changes by species. The brief was to design a free app for 21 to 28 year-olds that brings all of this into one place and stays easy to use every day, for common pets from dogs and cats to reptiles, birds, and rodents.

Solution

  • Onboarding: A short quiz asks one question per screen (name, species, breed, age) using tap-to-select cards and lists, so typing is limited to the pet's name.
  • Structure: One home screen with four large entry points (Profile, Pet-friendly places, Health log, Reminders), plus a home button on every screen, puts each main section one tap away.
  • Health tracking: Weight and hygiene logs (oral care, bathing, nails) pair a quick "log now" action with past entries and a trend graph, so owners can see changes over time.
  • Safety: The symptom checklist tells users to see their vet as soon as possible if they select anything, so the app informs without trying to diagnose.
  • Local discovery: Pet-friendly places are grouped into six categories (clinics, groomers, daycares, stores, public areas, restaurants) on photo cards.

Contributions

  • Information architecture: Built the navigation map that organizes the app into onboarding, home, health log, pet-friendly places, reminders, and profile.
  • Wireframes: Wireframed each flow in low fidelity to settle layout and navigation before visual design.
  • UI design: Designed all 32 screens in Figma: layout, spacing, hierarchy, and components such as selection cards, list rows, trend charts, and bottom navigation. I built them with the brand assets Claudia created.
  • Prototype: Linked the screens into a clickable Figma prototype covering the full flow from sign-up to every section.

Problem

The original ITML Institute landing page relied on dense text, overlapping imagery, and inconsistent visual hierarchy, making the institute's programs difficult to scan. Important information—including the ITMLS certification—was hard to discover, and visitors had few clear calls to action.

Solution

I redesigned the page in Figma around a clearer content structure, more readable typography, and distinct sections for the institute, its certifications, and professional development opportunities. The redesign gives all three certifications equal visibility, introduces a career progression timeline, and uses consistent calls to action to make program exploration more intuitive.

Landing Page Wireframe

The wireframe established a more focused hero, scannable content cards, and dedicated sections for the three ITML certifications.

Design Process

01 — Website Evaluation

I reviewed the existing landing page to identify issues with content organization, readability, navigation, and visual consistency, including the missing ITMLS certification and unclear next steps.

02 — Content Restructuring

I reorganized the page into distinct sections for the institute's goals, certification programs, professional development opportunities, and credibility indicators. A career progression timeline helps explain how the certifications relate to stages in an IT professional's career.

03 — UI Redesign in Figma

I refined the typography, spacing, alignment, color distribution, and component consistency. The updated interface uses structured cards, clearer content groups, and alternating backgrounds while retaining the institute's navy, blue, orange, and yellow palette and established visual identity.

Outcome

The result is a more organized Figma landing page redesign that presents all three certifications with equal visibility, clearer career progression, and more consistent paths for visitors to explore training opportunities.

Key Takeaways

This project strengthened my ability to evaluate an existing interface, identify usability issues, and translate those findings into practical improvements to information hierarchy, content discovery, and visual consistency.

Problem

I wanted to explore how Templo's visual identity could translate into a cohesive digital experience. The challenge was to create a website concept that conveyed the delicacy and elegance of its swimwear without overwhelming the products with unnecessary visual elements.

The design needed a clear visual hierarchy, complementary typography, and a cohesive color palette to establish a consistent visual direction across the homepage and product sections.

Solution

I developed a minimalist, flat-design website concept featuring a soft pink palette, refined typography, and a clean layout. By keeping decorative elements to a minimum, I aimed to give the swimwear visual prominence while creating an elegant and cohesive browsing experience.

I began by developing the visual concept in Figma, defining the layout, typography, and color palette. I also explored translating the design into HTML and CSS using Visual Studio Code.

Graphic Concept & Design Criteria

Visual Direction

The design follows a minimalist, flat-design approach intended to complement the elegance and delicacy of Templo's swimwear. A restrained use of graphic elements keeps the focus on the products, while the overall layout aims to create a clean and balanced presentation.

Design Process

01 — Visual Exploration

I established the visual direction by selecting a minimalist design approach, defining the color palette, and pairing Garamond with Open Sans. These decisions formed the foundation for a consistent visual language.

02 — UI Design in Figma

I developed the website concept in Figma, exploring the homepage and product sections. I focused on layout, typography, color, and visual hierarchy to create a cohesive presentation aligned with the brand's aesthetic.

03 — HTML & CSS Exploration

Using Visual Studio Code, I explored translating the visual concept into HTML and CSS. This allowed me to connect my design process with front-end fundamentals and better understand how a visual layout can be represented through code.

Outcome

The project resulted in a Figma-based website concept that translates Templo's swimwear aesthetic into a minimalist digital design. It provided an opportunity to strengthen my visual design skills and explore the relationship between interface design and front-end development.

Key Takeaway

This project helped me develop a more structured approach to web design, from establishing a visual direction to applying typography, color, and hierarchy across multiple sections. It also encouraged me to explore how design decisions translate into HTML and CSS.

Problem

The agency needed a dedicated podcast booking page within its existing website. The challenge was to create a clear, professional experience that communicated the podcast's value, organized essential information, and guided prospective guests toward booking a session.

Beyond the visual design, the page needed to support a functional booking and payment process without requiring a redesign of the existing website.

Solution

I designed and built a dedicated podcast booking experience using UI/UX principles and WordPress with Elementor. I organized the page content, prepared the podcast thumbnails and supporting assets, and created a visual flow to guide visitors from discovering the podcast to booking a session.

To bring the experience into production, I embedded Acuity Scheduling and integrated Stripe payments, connecting the page's design with the functionality needed to manage bookings.

Problem

After four levels of running and jumping, the game needed a finale that felt different. The boss level had to teach a new ability quickly, build tension, and make damage easy to read.

Solution

  • Onboarding: A text box explains that yarn affects the boss and clicking throws it.
  • Pacing: A calm corridor with chill music leads into a dungeon as the metal guitar solos begin.
  • Risk and reward: Mia fires lasers when you're close, encouraging you to fight from range.
  • Feedback: This is the only level with health bars above both the player and the boss.
  • Resolution: At 0%, Mia loops a white-flag surrender; you then reach the last door and the game ends.

Contributions

  • Level design: Designed Level 5's layout, the corridor into the boss fight, the arena, and the player's path from introduction to final exit.
  • Boss design: Designed Mia as the final boss, including her laser attack, yarn weakness, health system, and defeat state.
  • 2D art: Designed Mia and created her sprites, Aseprite animations, and defeat animation, plus game-ready assets adapted from the team's Illustrator artwork.
  • Implementation: Built the boss fight in Godot, including the laser, yarn damage, health behavior, and 0% defeat state, then tested and iterated on it.
